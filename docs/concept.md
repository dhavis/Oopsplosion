# Oopsplosion - concept brief

Source: product conversation (Hila + Alex, 20 Aug 2026). Extra-damage / "I did that" authorship added 28 Aug 2026. This is the starting point for concept and gameability evaluation. It is not a locked design bible. Agents should challenge it.

## Player intent

A phone game about smashing things to vent. The player already knows rage-room games exist. She wants a different vibe: crazier, more satisfying, not "tap to break a glass."

She wants two feelings in the same round:

- **The chain:** one touch starts a spiral that goes past what she planned.
- **Extra damage:** she can still personally wreck more, and those hits should feel like **"I did that"** — authored, not watched.

Spectatorship after the fuse is not enough. Additional damage is a required second layer, not a nice-to-have.

## What this game is not

Not a prettier Pocket Rage Room. The market already has:

- Classic rage rooms (room + objects + smash)
- Simple smash-an-object loops with coins and upgrades
- Heavy destruction sandboxes (too big for a 5-minute phone session)
- Combo plus daily-challenge smash games (Rage Smash and similar)

Players already complain that some rage rooms end too fast and you cannot really wreck the whole space. Copying that category loses.

Additional damage is not a return to classic rage room. If extra hits are leftover-object cleanup, or a hammer tray, this is Pocket Rage Room with a chain trailer. Extra damage must stay a second authorship layer on top of a spiral that still has to exist.

## Positioning

Sell two satisfactions in one round: creating a spiral, and personally dealing extra damage.

The chain is **"not my fault."** The extra hits are **"I did that."** Do not ship one without the other.

The player does not receive a room and an order to destroy it. The player receives an annoying situation, lights a fuse, and can also pile on. The spiral is still the product sentence. Extra damage is the player's own wreckage inside that sentence.

Working pitch lines from the source conversation (not the product name):

- "You make things happen."
- "MAKE IT WORSE."
- Chain-reaction rage, not rage room.
- "I did that."

Product name is Oopsplosion. Logo may style as Oops-plosion. Do not rename the product to Make It Worse or Not My Problem without an explicit product decision.

## Core fantasy

An everyday irritation becomes a physics cartoon. One move should feel like it was the player's idea. Then the world escalates past what they planned. The player can also cause additional damage themselves — extra hits that juice as theirs: impact, authorship, **"I did that."** Slow motion. Screen shake. A score that jokes about how badly the situation went.

## Core loop

1. Situation loads calm. Everything is ordered. The annoyance is readable in 2 seconds.
2. A constraint or tool is given (or a short timer).
3. Player chooses a first touch - the fuse, not a checklist of objects to smash.
4. Physics and authored reactions chain. The spiral runs.
5. Player can cause additional damage — extra hits they choose, on objects they mean. Those hits must feel personally authored (**"I did that"**), distinct from the chain (**"not my fault"**). Additional damage is in addition to the chain, not a replacement for it. Open: during the chain, after it, or both. Design must pick; the fantasy requires the feeling either way.
6. The best chains freeze for a beat: slow-mo, punchline, score.
7. Player retries the same situation for a better chain, chaos, punchline, or a meaner extra hit.

The chain is the game. Extra damage is the player's own hits. Destruction is juice for both.

## Situation examples

Traffic jam. Endless queue. A car cuts in. Horns. "47 minutes." Tools might include hammer, wrecking ball, giant fist, paint bucket, water balloon, laser, magnet, mini tornado, God finger. A car crush flies into a sign, the sign hits a light, traffic lurches, a truck hits a puddle, a stall collapses, and so on.

Sunday at the office. Stuck printer, spilled coffee, frozen PC, ringing phone, copier that will not stop, wobbly chair, hanging lamp. Goal is not "break the office." Goal is a 20-second chain: coffee -> printer -> paper -> fan -> lamp -> chair -> copier. The player can still personally knock extra objects (the plant, the mug, the phone) and feel they did that. Those extra hits are not the round's purpose.

Daily revenge (habit layer). One new irritation per day, about 30 seconds to turn it into chaos:

- Phone rings the second you fall asleep
- Printer fails again
- Light turns red as you arrive
- Package delivered to the neighbor
- Washer ate a sock

## Puzzle constraints

Not every level is free smash. Constraints turn a sandbox into a game:

- Do not touch anything (make the system destroy itself)
- Only one rubber band
- No hands
- Collapse the room with a single object
- You have 30 seconds
- Only 3 objects allowed

Infinite solutions per situation is the replay hook.

Constraints can still limit extra damage (one poke, one object, hands off the annoyance). They must not erase the "I did that" layer on every round. At least some play has to let the player land extra hits that are clearly theirs.

## Scoring (draft)

Not "how many things you broke" as the only score. Score the quality of the spiral, and keep extra authored damage visible as the player's hits:

- Damage: how much the situation actually broke
- Precision: did the intended fuse work
- Combos / chain: length and elegance of the reaction
- Chaos: how out of control it got
- Escalation: did it get worse, not just louder
- Satisfaction: juice, timing, punchline
- Authored extra damage ("I did that"): wreckage from the player's own extra hits, not from the chain. The player should be able to point at something and know it was their hit, not the copier's.

On-screen joke stats from the source conversation:

- CHAOS: 97%
- SATISFACTION: 112%
- THINGS THAT DEFINITELY WEREN'T YOUR FAULT: 43

End-card voice: "THAT ESCALATED QUICKLY."

THINGS THAT DEFINITELY WEREN'T YOUR FAULT is the chain's joke. Extra damage must not disappear into that number, or "I did that" has no credit.

## Virality (draft)

Clips people would watch:

- "Destroy this office with ONE move"
- "Most satisfying kitchen demolition"
- "Only 3 objects allowed"
- Longest chain leaderboard

The share moment is the chain, not the hammer swing. Extra damage is an in-hand feeling. It does not have to be the clip.

## Gameability questions agents must answer

1. Is there a clear goal each round, or is it still a sandbox?
2. Do constraints create interesting choices, or busywork?
3. Can a first-time player get a chain in one attempt?
4. Does retrying the same situation feel like mastery, not grinding?
5. Can this run on a phone in about 5 minutes without looking cheap?
6. What is the minimum content set that proves the fantasy?
7. What would make someone say "one more situation"?
8. Does additional damage feel like "I did that," or leftover smash?
9. Can the player tell their extra hits from the chain's wreckage?
10. When do extra hits happen — during the chain, after it, or both — without killing the watch moment?
